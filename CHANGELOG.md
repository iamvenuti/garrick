# Changelog

What changed in each release. Dates are when the release was tagged. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and versions follow [semantic versioning](https://semver.org/).

## [Unreleased]

### Changed

- **The README and the deck say which commits the wall check covers.** Both said "a check runs before every commit", but the check runs in each zone, on the commits of your work, and the wikis' repository has none. They now say every commit of your work is checked against the walls.

### Fixed

- **Ask's question about a name can be answered with a zone, or with the option as it was offered** ([#294](https://github.com/iamvenuti/garrick/issues/294)). A name that fits two entries gets one question back, but the answer could settle it only with another name: "Which do you mean: Pricing in Work or Pricing in Personal?" answered "The one in Work." asked the same question again, every time, and so did "In Work" or "Acme Corp" for a person listed under two tags of one company, which only the bare tag could settle. `ask_scope.mentions()` now also settles a name by the zone the answer names, and by one of the options exactly as the question said it, "Dana Whitlock of Acme Corp in Work", the longest where one option holds another. Every way the request narrows a name must agree. An answer that names two of the options, or none of them, gets the question again, and nothing is read or sent until one settles it; "Acme Corp" names both of those options, so it is asked again too. A zone counts only in the answer to the question, so "what did Dana say about work?" is still asked about.
- **A wall reset after a question back keeps the question it answered** ([#294](https://github.com/iamvenuti/garrick/issues/294)). With Acme's records held, "Where does Pricing stand?" asked which Pricing, and "The Birch one." started afresh at the wall with the answer alone, so neither new session saw the question. The request the box asked about now goes on past the reset, to the command session as `<earlier-request>` with its names and to the answer session with the answer, so the reply is about Birch's Pricing. It goes on only when nothing in it stands for a party on the far side of the wall, as the new `ask_scope.far_side()` reads it, more widely than names are settled: a name settled to a far party; anywhere, in any case, with or without accents and inside a longer word, a far party's name, tag or mail domain, a far person's name or an alias of either; and as a word of its own, a first name written small, a surname alone, or a far project's or thread's name or spoken form. A name settled to the near side is kept, though the far side has one like it. Otherwise the request is left out whole, since what it was about belongs to the far side as much as its names do, and the new sessions get the answer alone, as before. The reset that runs when the records to read, not the request, cross the wall applies the same test: it gave the fresh answer session the earlier request whatever it named. A reply whose request went on carries `"carried": true` beside `"reset": "wall"`, and the app puts its divider above the question asked about, so what is below it is what the new session was given.

## [0.49.0] - 2026-10-31

Ask answers questions from your records, inside one scope and sealed from everything else; Garrick's scheduled jobs take `gr-` names and one nightly job does what four did; the Checks card acts on each finding; Settings chooses the graph's colours and the cmux tab's assistant; and the wall check reads links and paths into another project.

### Added

- **Ask answers from your records, inside one scope, and starts afresh at a wall** ([#261](https://github.com/iamvenuti/garrick/issues/261), slice C). With the preview `ask` on, a question about what your records hold, "what did Dana say about the pallet quote?" or "where does Pricing stand?", gets an answer read from them. Before any session hears the request, `ask.py` settles its names against the list of projects and threads and the Parties, People and Aliases of `System/context.md`; a name that fits two entries gets one question back, with `kind: "clarify"`, and nothing is read. The command session then proposes a `find`, a route (Meetings, Knowledge, a thread, Garrick's docs) and the words to look for, and the new `ask_scope.py` reads the files inside that scope only, a project's `Sources/` documents through `extract.py` among them; no session gets a read tool. It reads nothing whose parties it cannot state in full: a meeting page needs a zone and a list of known tags, a person or entity page its `party` alone, a project file is of its hub's party and its thread's, a project whose hub names no party is refused, and a Knowledge page that carries a party or a zone is skipped. A second session answers from the passages on `GARRICK_ASK_ANSWER_TIER`, `sonnet` unless set, started with the first question, with its own brief, `ask-answer-brief.md`, and nothing else: no settings but the deny profile, no memory, and an empty folder of its own outside the workspace; what it says is shown and never carried out, so a record can never add an action. The find and its answer are one call against the caps and one ledger line. `Asker.admit()` is the only way a record reaches a prompt, and it records the parties of what it admits; a request for a party walled from one held, or a question across every zone while anything is held, stops both sessions before anything is read, clears what they were told, a confirmation waiting for a yes among it, and starts fresh with the new request alone, the reply carrying `"reset": "wall"`. A question across every zone reads within one group of parties no wall divides, and ends with the rules' sentence when a wall held records back. With Codex a question gets a plain reply that answers need Claude. Every reply now carries `kind` (`command`, `clarify` or `answer`), and `Asker.progress` reports the steps of an answer. `ask.log` no longer holds request or answer text: the time, the kind, the milliseconds and the tokens, unless `GARRICK_ASK_LOG_TEXT=1`.
- **Choose the colour of each kind of note in Explore, in Settings** ([#291](https://github.com/iamvenuti/garrick/issues/291)). The graph's colours were fixed in `status.py`, so changing one meant editing a shipped file that the next update put back. Settings has a new section, *Graph colours*, with a row for each kind the legend can show, any kind found from a wiki's pages included. Each row uses the palette *Zones* uses, with *Default*, the shipped colour, as its first cell, and without Red, Crimson and Amber, which the graph's rings use. A chosen colour draws in its light shade in the light theme and its dark shade in the dark one, on the graph, in its legend and in a note's links. *Reset colours* puts every kind back. Closing Settings saves the section beside the zones in `System/garrick-settings.json`, as `"graph": {"project": "Teal"}`, through the `settings` verb, which refuses an unknown kind or colour; `garrick_lib` keeps `graph` with the other choices, so an update leaves it alone. Also, a colour or an icon picked from a palette in *Zones* now counts as a change by itself, so closing Settings saves it even when nothing else changed.
- **Choose whether a cmux tab starts Claude Code or Codex, in Settings** ([#279](https://github.com/iamvenuti/garrick/issues/279)). The cmux extra's `agent` setting could only be changed by editing `System/desk.json`, so a Codex user got tabs that started `claude`. With the extra installed and *Open in cmux* switched on, Settings › *Opening a thread* now has *A cmux tab starts* under it: Claude Code or Codex. Closing Settings has the desk write the choice to `System/desk.json`, through the new `desk agent [claude|codex]`, which keeps every other setting there, so a tab opened from the app, a menu or by voice starts the same assistant. The `setup` skill sets it from what the probe finds: `codex` alone gives Codex, `claude` alone Claude Code, both asks. Before it opens a tab, desk checks that the assistant's command is installed, on its PATH, in the folders the two assistants install to, or as your own shell finds it, and when it is not, says so and opens nothing instead of a tab whose command fails. A wrap sent to a tab is still worded for the assistant running in it.

### Changed

- **The Checks card acts on its findings, and says what its two buttons do** ([#278](https://github.com/iamvenuti/garrick/issues/278)). The findings are sorted by Nurture's kinds, walls, mechanical, attribution and judgment, so the list and the context debt above it use one vocabulary, and the largest group is open, so the link from *Needs attention* no longer lands on a column of closed headings. Each finding's path opens the file. A mechanical finding on a note in a zone has *Fix*, Nurture's own fix for that note, as on a thread row's check chip, and any other mechanical finding *Nurture*; a wall has *Take it out* and an attribution *Answer*, each handing the request that nurtures its zone or wiki to your assistant; a judgment is the file to open. Under *Nurture Work*, one line says what a pass will do there: how many fixes on one yes, questions one at a time, and findings for your judgment. Under it, what the last run fixed, had answered and left, and that the debt is that run's while the list is this build's quick check. *Review context* says what it does, and how many items the latest review fixed, answered and deferred: the `nurture` skill now keeps those three counts in the review record's frontmatter, and `check.py` warns when one is not a whole number. `nurture.py` gains `check_items()`, `sort_item()` and `day_outcome()`, which the page reads. A finding that cannot get *Fix* is sorted without reading its history, so a rebuild costs no git call for it.
- **Ask's session starts with the Garrick App, and stays out of sight** ([#261](https://github.com/iamvenuti/garrick/issues/261), slice A). With the preview `ask` on, the Mac app starts the Ask box's assistant session a second after its window shows, so the first request no longer waits for a cold start, and the window waits for none of it. The app sends `ask.py keep` then and every two minutes: it starts the session when it is not running and never replaces a live one, and while the app holds it, a session that died is started again within fifteen seconds, with one line in `ask.log` that holds no request or answer. Quitting the app stops the server and its session; a crash or a force quit stops them within ten minutes, when the app's lease lapses; and switching the preview off stops them at the app's next check. The session is no longer anybody's work session: every process the server starts carries `GARRICK_BACKGROUND`, so the notice hooks write no turn, flush nothing held and send no *Finished* or *Needs you* for it; it carries no `CLAUDECODE` or `CMUX_*` variable from the tab `ask.py` may have been started in, and it runs the real `claude` as *Continue* finds it, never a terminal's wrapper, so cmux has no record of it. `ask.py status` reports `leased`. And a new session is given the names list: after *reset*, after the box's opening replaced a session older than three hours, or after a restart, the new process was sent `<workspace unchanged/>` and no names at all, so it could not resolve a project or a thread until the list itself changed. The list now goes to every new process with its first request.
- **The Ask box is one window that keeps its exchange, with Stop** ([#261](https://github.com/iamvenuti/garrick/issues/261), slice B). The hotkey brings the same window forward and never opens a second. Its field takes several lines: Return sends, Shift-Return starts a new line. Escape hides the window, and what was asked and answered stays in it until *New conversation*, which also starts the assistant on a fresh session, or until the app quits, however long the window was hidden. A command keeps the window compact, its answer under the field; while a request runs, a line says what is happening, *Preparing the assistant…* when the session is still starting, with *Stop*. `claude -p` documents no way to interrupt a turn, so *Stop* ends the session process: nothing the request would have done is done, the question goes back into the field, and the next request starts a new session, given the whole list. The window grows for an answer or a question back, and keeps an earlier exchange under a divider when a wall made the assistant start afresh, ready for the slices that bring answers. Underneath, a request's reply is JSON lines on its one connection: none or more `{"progress": …}`, then one final line that always carries `kind` (`command`, `clarify` or `answer`). `ask.py --json` prints each line as it comes, and the app reads them as they arrive. An interrupt to `ask.py`, the app's *Stop* or Ctrl-C, says `{"stop": true}` on that connection; the final line then says `"stopped": true`, and the ledger counts the stopped turn as a call. CI now type-checks `Garrick.swift` where the runner has a Swift compiler.
- **Ask's command session is sealed, as its answer session is** ([#261](https://github.com/iamvenuti/garrick/issues/261)). The command session ran in the workspace with `--setting-sources user,project`, so it loaded your `CLAUDE.md`, the workspace's rules and context, and your assistant's memory, which can hold a line about every thread on both sides of every wall. It now loads no settings but the deny profile (`--setting-sources ""`), runs with `CLAUDE_CODE_DISABLE_AUTO_MEMORY=1`, and works in an empty folder of its own outside the workspace, made when it starts and removed when it stops, however it started: with the app, on `warm`, after `reset` or a wall reset, or started again after it died. It is still told only names and states. The aliases and people it read from `System/context.md` now come with each request instead, as a `<names>` block: each name the request holds, as heard, and what it was settled to, a party, a person and their party, a project or a thread, so "birchen co" or a person's first name still routes; `ask-brief.md` describes it. With Codex, each request's `codex exec` runs in an empty folder outside the workspace, removed when it returns, so it finds no `AGENTS.md` of the workspace's.
- **The Scheduled jobs card says what each job does and whose it is** ([#273](https://github.com/iamvenuti/garrick/issues/273)). A job's plist can give one line in `EnvironmentVariables` as `GARRICK_ABOUT`, shown under its name; every plist Garrick ships has one, and a plist without it shows nothing in its place. The card lists Garrick's jobs, those whose label starts `garrick.`, apart from *Yours*; a job with a heartbeat and no plist is one a hook or a session starts, and says *started by a session* instead of *schedule not found*. The name no longer opens the log through `file://`, which on a Mac opened Console, often behind the window: it opens a panel under the row with what the job does, its schedule, its label, whether it calls the assistant, its last runs and the last 30 lines of its log, and *Open log*, which the Mac app opens in the app this Mac uses for plain text, TextEdit failing that, instead of Console. A job should therefore log counts and paths, never an assistant's answer or a note's text. Install and update never touch `~/Library/LaunchAgents`, so a plist already loaded shows no description until it is copied in again; the job rename later in this release does that for Garrick's. [Scheduled jobs](docs/extras/scheduled-jobs.md) says how to describe a job of your own.
- **One nightly job: nurture, the effort record, your own checks and the page** ([#276](https://github.com/iamvenuti/garrick/issues/276)). `garrick.nurture` becomes `gr-nightly`, run by the new `extras/jobs/nightly.py` at 05:15 through `job.py`, with no assistant. It runs four steps in order, each whatever happened in the one before: Context Nurture's mechanical fixes as before, with `--scope` and `--digest` (the night's only full `check.py`); `effort.py --record`, with the `--also` and `--moved` the plist gives it, so the Effort view keeps the days Claude Code's transcripts lose after 30; the workspace's own checks in `System/jobs/nightly.local`, when that file exists and is executable, which Garrick never ships or changes and whose absence is no fault (one that is there but not executable is skipped, and the job's row turns amber); and `status.py --same-flags`, built even when a step before it failed, since the page is where the failure shows. Each step has its own time limit, so one that hangs is stopped and the rest run; the plist gives the job `--timeout 3600`. Each step runs in a process group of its own, which is stopped whole at its limit, so nothing a step started, git included, goes on outside the job's lock; its output reaches the log as it comes. When the job itself is stopped (`launchctl bootout`, a logout, a shutdown), `nightly.py` stops the step under way within two seconds, before `job.py` would kill it, and records the step's process group in the jobs folder, with the time the step started, so `job.py` stops that group too even if `nightly.py` is killed outright, and only while its first process is still that step; a record left by a run that died some other way is taken away, never acted on; a step that ignores SIGTERM is not left behind. The job exits 1 when any step failed, and 0 otherwise. A command can now report its steps to `job.py` (`agent.report_steps()`, through `GARRICK_STEPS_FILE`): the heartbeat keeps each step's exit code, seconds and last lines as `steps`, a failure's reason names the step and its own code ("exited 1 after 61s; nightly.local (exit 2) failed"), and the job's panel lists them. The page the job builds as its last step comes before its heartbeat, so it reads the steps of the run under way, and a step that failed that night already puts the job in red and under *Needs attention*. The 23:30 page rebuild, `extras/status/garrick.status.plist`, is no longer shipped: the nightly job rebuilds the page. No assistant call is added: the nightly job makes none, so the jobs' caps of 12 calls an hour and 48 a day are spent only by the sweeps and the brief, as before. A job of your own that ran `effort.py --record`, rebuilt the page or ran checks of yours each night can go: move the checks into `nightly.local` and unload it. [Scheduled jobs](docs/extras/scheduled-jobs.md#the-nightly-job) describes the job, and the [Garrick App](docs/extras/status-page.md) page no longer asks you to schedule the effort record yourself.
- **Garrick's own jobs have names that start with `gr-`, so they read apart from yours** ([#275](https://github.com/iamvenuti/garrick/issues/275)). The name a job runs under is what its log, heartbeat, lock, notices, *Run now* and row on the jobs card show, and Garrick's were plain words a job of yours could have. Each plist's label is now `garrick.` and the job's name, so one gives the other:

  | Before | Now | Label now |
  |---|---|---|
  | `nurture` | `gr-nightly` | `garrick.gr-nightly` |
  | `status` (the 23:30 rebuild) | part of `gr-nightly` | none: set aside as `garrick.status.plist.retired` |
  | `todo-sweep` | `gr-todo-sweep` | `garrick.gr-todo-sweep` |
  | `reading-sweep` | `gr-reading-sweep` | `garrick.gr-reading-sweep` |
  | `whats-open` | `gr-whats-open` | `garrick.gr-whats-open` |
  | `backup` | `gr-backup` | `garrick.gr-backup` |

  The shipped plists in `System/jobs/launchd/` carry the new names, and the old files there stay, as an update never deletes. Your copies in `~/Library/LaunchAgents` are yours and keep working under the old names; the update changes nothing there and runs nothing. For each one that runs a job of Garrick's for this workspace under its old name, the update's report names the plist and prints the lines to run in Terminal, for example for the backup:

  ```sh
  launchctl bootout gui/$(id -u)/garrick.backup
  mv -n ~/Library/LaunchAgents/garrick.backup.plist ~/Library/LaunchAgents/garrick.gr-backup.plist
  plutil -replace Label -string garrick.gr-backup ~/Library/LaunchAgents/garrick.gr-backup.plist
  plutil -replace ProgramArguments -json '["/usr/bin/python3", "/Users/you/Garrick/System/jobs/job.py", "gr-backup", "--cwd", "/Users/you/Garrick", "--", "/usr/bin/python3", "/Users/you/Garrick/System/jobs/backup.py", "--workspace", "/Users/you/Garrick"]' ~/Library/LaunchAgents/garrick.gr-backup.plist
  plutil -replace StandardOutPath -string ~/Library/Logs/garrick-jobs/gr-backup.launchd.log ~/Library/LaunchAgents/garrick.gr-backup.plist
  plutil -replace StandardErrorPath -string ~/Library/Logs/garrick-jobs/gr-backup.launchd.log ~/Library/LaunchAgents/garrick.gr-backup.plist
  plutil -replace EnvironmentVariables -json '{"GARRICK_ABOUT": "Commits and pushes every repository, holding back what the wall check refuses.", "PATH": "/opt/homebrew/bin:/usr/local/bin:/usr/bin:/bin", "GARRICK_QUIET_EXITS": "5 6", "GARRICK_RENAMED_FROM": "backup"}' ~/Library/LaunchAgents/garrick.gr-backup.plist
  launchctl bootstrap gui/$(id -u) ~/Library/LaunchAgents/garrick.gr-backup.plist
  ```

  The report gives each plist its own lines, with its own label, paths and arguments as you edited them. Each line sets a whole value, on the moved file, so a block pasted twice, or stopped half way and run again, ends as a block run once does, and a plist left part way through is reported again with what is still to do. For `garrick.nurture` the arguments also point at `nightly.py` and set `--timeout 3600`, in place of any time limit it had; for `garrick.status` the report gives only `launchctl bootout gui/$(id -u)/garrick.status` and `mv -n ~/Library/LaunchAgents/garrick.status.plist ~/Library/LaunchAgents/garrick.status.plist.retired`, to run once `gr-nightly` is loaded: launchd does not load a `.retired` file, and you may delete it. `plutil` keeps your other edits and drops the plist's opening XML comment. Nothing in the jobs folder is moved. Only what was Garrick's moves to the new name: the commands set `GARRICK_RENAMED_FROM` to the old name in the renamed plist, and only then do `job.py status` and the Garrick App read the old name's heartbeat, logs and assistant calls from before the rename (the first run under the new name, or before it the renamed plist's last change) under the new name, so its run strip keeps its two weeks and the old name gets no row of its own. A run under the old name after that, such as a job of yours called `backup` run by cron or a hook, is not Garrick's: it keeps its own row, red when it fails. The evening rebuild's row goes once `gr-nightly` is there, as a plist or a heartbeat, no plist runs `status`, and no `status` run has finished since the nightly's first run; deleting the `.retired` file changes nothing, and its runs are never the nightly's. The jobs card counts `gr-` names and labels as Garrick's, and still counts `garrick.` labels as Garrick's before the rename. `gr-` is reserved for Garrick's jobs: `job.py` runs a job of yours named `gr-…` but says, on standard error and in its log, that the name reads as Garrick's. The jobs log folder keeps its name, `~/Library/Logs/garrick-jobs`.
- **Explore no longer reads Obsidian's graph colours** ([#292](https://github.com/iamvenuti/garrick/issues/292)). Settings › Explore's *Use Obsidian's graph colours* coloured a zone with the colour groups in its vault's `.obsidian/graph.json`, a file Obsidian rewrites and the installer leaves out of git, so the graph looked different depending on whether Obsidian was installed and how each vault was set up: a vault with no groups turned grey, a zone outside a vault and every wiki ignored the switch. The switch is gone, with the code that read `graph.json`, its parser for Obsidian's query syntax and the *Colours from Obsidian* line under the legend. Every place is coloured by its kinds, with Obsidian or without it, and *Reset view* resets only *Follow the zone in view*. A vault's *Excluded files* (`.obsidian/app.json`) still stay off the graph, since they say what to draw, not how. What a window saved for the old switch is ignored and dropped. [Obsidian](docs/extras/obsidian.md#making-the-graph-readable) says its colour groups colour Obsidian's own graph only.
- **The docs say what *Needs attention* collects** ([#277](https://github.com/iamvenuti/garrick/issues/277)). [The Garrick App](docs/extras/status-page.md#what-it-shows) lists what puts a line on it, red and amber, and why its count differs from the Checks card's: all of the check's errors make one line, and the context debt is weighed from the full check, the wiki lint and the shared-file comparison.

### Fixed

- **The wall check reads links and paths into a walled project or thread.** It now refuses a link, or a path written as code, into a note of a project walled from the note's own, and Nurture no longer turns such a path into a link or points a link after a note moved across a wall.
- **The checkpoint chip describes recorded work, not the current session's** ([#280](https://github.com/iamvenuti/garrick/issues/280)). Its tooltip said "Work this session recorded at …", but any session may have written the checkpoint, often one gone since, and a session open on the thread holds none of that conversation. It now reads "Work recorded at 20:05 on 30 October 2026 that no wrap has folded in yet".
- **Ask has call limits of its own, and stops spending the scheduled jobs'** ([#282](https://github.com/iamvenuti/garrick/issues/282)). Every Ask request counted against the jobs' limits, twelve calls an hour and 48 a day across every job on the Mac, so about a dozen requests in an hour stopped the box, and a morning of asking could leave the night's jobs refused. Ask's requests now count against `GARRICK_CAP_ASK_HOUR` (60), `GARRICK_CAP_ASK_DAY` (300) and `GARRICK_CAP_ASK_COST_DAY` (20 dollars), and the jobs' limits count the jobs' calls alone. Both stay in the one ledger, Ask's as job `ask`. At one of Ask's limits the box says so in plain words and says when it can answer again, "Ask has reached its limit of 60 requests an hour. It can answer again at 15:31.", in place of an error that named variables the app cannot set; `ask.log` marks the request `capped`. The *Assistant calls* card shows Ask's meters beside the jobs', which it then labels *Job calls*. In `agent.py`, `caps(budget)` reads the jobs' or Ask's limits, `budget_of(job)` says which a call counts against, and `cap_reached()` says which limit stops the next call and when it clears; `run()` applies the budget of the job it is given. A scheduled job can no longer be named `ask`, so none can spend Ask's budget, and a cap set below zero refuses every call rather than failing.
- **Ask's reply to a command says its sentence once, and `ask.log` says a request was stopped** ([#283](https://github.com/iamvenuti/garrick/issues/283)). "open Pricing in Claude" showed *Opening Pricing in Claude.* and the same words again under it. The app now leaves out a line of what was done that matches the sentence, case and a final full stop aside. A request ended with *Stop* was logged as `not ok`; its `ask.log` line now says `stopped`.

## [0.48.0] - 2026-10-31

Nurture runs a guided context review that goes on where the last one stopped, started by voice or from the Checks card; a question the wikis raise is a file of its own; and the docs call the status page the Garrick App and draw the architecture in four layers.

### Added

- **A guided context review.** "Review the context with me", or "review the latest audit with me", has the `nurture` skill work through what the checks left in a short session: it gathers the check, the wiki lint, the shared-file comparison and any audit report the workspace keeps, checks each finding again, and asks walls and failed sources first, then one batch of mechanical fixes, then one decision at a time, up to a limit you choose, fifteen minutes or ten questions by default. `System/nurture-review.md` keeps the decisions, so the next review goes on where this one stopped. It sits in the root repository, which no wall hook checks, so it holds no material: each item is the finding's key, twelve hex digits that `nurture.py --json` now gives every finding, so two findings on one file are two items; a decision is `fixed` and its commit, `answered`, `deferred to` a date, `skipped` or `source failed`, never the answer; a reason is one of the skill's fixed few; and a wall is never settled, only fixed or deferred. `check.py` warns about a row outside that form. The Garrick App's Checks card has *Review context*, which hands the request to your assistant, beside the day of the last review and whether one is unfinished, read from that file's frontmatter alone. ([#258](https://github.com/iamvenuti/garrick/issues/258))
- **A question from the wikis is a file** ([#224](https://github.com/iamvenuti/garrick/issues/224), point 1). When an ingest or a lint found something only you could settle, such as who decides or which of two figures holds, it ended up as a line under an *Open* heading on the page, and nothing read it back. The `meetings` and `knowledge` skills now write it as `Wikis/<Wiki>/questions/<YYMMDD> - <slug>.md`, in the form of a *Needs you* question (`type: question`, `asked`, `status`, the bold line, why it matters, the options), with `origin:`, the raw record that raised it, and `pages:`, the pages the answer would change, and leave the page as it is. A Meetings question also carries `zone:` and `parties:`; a Knowledge question carries neither, as nothing in Knowledge does. Both wikis' templates gain the `questions/` folder, and each schema describes the file. `check.py` holds a wiki question to the same rules as a *Needs you* question, through the same code, under its *Questions* heading, and warns about a Meetings question with no zone or parties, an unknown zone, an unknown party or both sides of a wall, as it does a meeting page; Nurture asks a missing or unknown zone or party as attribution, and treats both sides of a wall as a wall finding, as for a meeting page. ## [0.30.0] - 2026-10-15

### Added

- A thread keeps its place even when the session never gets to say "wrap it". A wrap is what makes a thread resumable cold, and nothing but the user's own word starts one, so the sessions that end another way — a call, a closed lid, a crash, a context that ran out — have been leaving whatever they produced to be reconstructed from the files, or lost. As the work happens the assistant now writes a checkpoint: a `### Checkpoint` block in the thread note, directly under Resume here, holding what this session made, what was decided and in whose words, what is only being considered, what is left half done, the actions still to file and a deadline where somebody gave one. Everything in it is labelled unconfirmed, with the time it covers, and only a wrap, a finish or an interrupt confirms anything: the Resume here block above it is untouched, so the one block every reader trusts still means what it meant. "Open X" reads both and says the checkpoint as unconfirmed; the next wrap folds it in, files its actions and removes the block. Three things fire one and nothing else: a file saved for the thread, a decision stated in words that decide, or the next action changing. Not a clock, not every turn, and not the user asking — "wrap it" and "hold this" stay their commands — and a long discussion with none of the three leaves no block at all. It never writes the zone's `Todo.md`, never commits, never runs the wall check and carries no meeting material, so a decision resting on a conversation waits for the wrap, where the wall check runs as it always has. One tool writes it, `scaffold.py checkpoint`, taking one JSON object on standard input, which is what keeps the shape, the sources and the repeat test out of habit's hands: every item names its source, a date is an ISO date somebody gave or the line says "no date given", a Considering item cannot be promoted into a decision, and the same state sent again writes nothing, ten seconds or ten hours later, because the comparison is against the note and not a clock. An item is changed by its id and removed only when asked, so a second session cannot lose the first one's work, and a write from a session that has not read the note is refused with what is there now. The block's extent is its two markers and nothing else: a block that ended at the next heading would swallow the dated entries below it, which are the thread's history. A hand-edited block is shown and reported, never overwritten and never resumed from. The status page carries a chip read from the block's comment line alone, "unwrapped since 14:32", or "checkpoint out of date" where a wrap has since moved the resume point, and orders Recent work by whichever is later, the note's `updated:` or the checkpoint's own time; no content of the block reaches the page, and the page gets no button, because confirming a checkpoint is a wrap. ## [0.7.0] - 2026-10-06

Updates: a newer Garrick now reaches a workspace you have already installed, without losing what you changed. And a first-run setup that looks at your Mac before it asks you anything.

### Added

- `install.py --update ~/Garrick` brings an installed workspace up to the Garrick you run it from, without losing what you changed. It reports first and changes nothing until `--apply`. The card stays read-only: nothing on it commits. Untracked files in a new folder are counted one by one.

### Fixed

- `todo_lines.py` closes the files it reads, so the tests no longer print `ResourceWarning`.
- The status page's Repositories card says a repository "could not be read" when `git status` fails or runs past 30 seconds, instead of showing it as all committed.

### Security

- The filing commands of the intake and meetings skills, and the meetings skill's person, index and log writes, check where a file will really land and refuse rather than file somewhere else. A symbolic link is followed only while it stays inside the folder that was checked, the project or the wiki; one that leads out of it, into another project or off the workspace, is refused. The file is then written relative to that folder, opened without following any link, so a link put on the way after the check makes the command refuse instead of writing through it. A mail refused partway through filing stays in its inbox, with no draft page left behind. Every such command has boundary tests. Details are in the security advisory published with this release.
