Worktops Compared: Oak, Laminate and Composite
By the Homewright test team. Published August 2026. Saved from the web, 19 September 2026.

We fitted three worktops in our test kitchen and used them hard for six months.

Oak is the warmest to look at and the most forgiving: scratches and burns sand out. It needs oiling about twice a year, and standing water marks it. Around 250 euros a metre, fitted.

Laminate is the cheapest, around 90 euros a metre fitted, and easy to wipe. Chips at the edges cannot be repaired; the length has to be replaced.

Composite, a resin-bound stone, came out hardest-wearing in our tests. It needs no oiling and resists stains, but a chip is a job for a specialist, and it is heavy to fit. Around 400 euros a metre, fitted.

Our verdict: composite if you want to forget about it, oak if you like a surface that ages and can be put right.
