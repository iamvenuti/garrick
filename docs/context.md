# How Garrick keeps context

What the assistant knows about your work is spread over many places in Garrick: the wikis, the thread notes, `System/context.md` and `System/names.md`, each zone's `Todo.md` and the assistant's own memory. This page shows how they fit together, in four stages. Each stage says what happens, which file or tool does it, and where the detail is. It repeats none of that detail.

![Context is all you need. Articles and reports are ingested into Knowledge, a graph of what you read, linked by topic. Recordings and mail are ingested into Meetings, a graph of what was said, linked by who and what. The content itself sits in two graphs and a tree. The two wikis are the graphs: what comes in is ingested into them, and their pages link to one another by topic, person and organisation. Your zones are the tree: folders by subject, a project inside a zone and a piece of work inside a project. The rest of this page is how the three are filled, kept true, used and let go.

All four stages work under one rule: [one memory, walled use](principles.md#one-memory-walled-use). Every conversation is kept in one place, so you can find your own record. The walls apply where material is *used*, not where it is stored. The zones and each zone's walled or open flag in `System/context.md` are what each stage reads, and the parties are the names it routes mail by. They are not a stage of their own.

> Each zone is either **walled** or **open**. What is in a walled zone stays in that zone; what is in an open zone may go anywhere. Knowledge and System reach every zone, and a conversation that touches two walled zones is split before it is filed.

| Stage | What it does | What does it |
|---|---|---|
| [Take in](#take-in) | Everything lands in an inbox and is filed by who it came from | The inboxes, the `intake` skill, the two wikis' `raw/` folders, the Parties table |
| [Keep true](#keep-true) | What the workspace holds stays correct, and says where it came from | Learned entries and their origin, `correct.py`, `check.py`, `wiki_lint.py`, `drift.py`, Nurture, the wrap |
| [Use within walls](#use-within-walls) | The right context reaches the task, and a confidence stays on its own side | The Resume here block, question routing, briefs, the wall check at use and at commit |
| [Let fade](#let-fade) | What nobody uses steps back, and can be brought back | The todo sweep's archive, park and finish, memory pointers set aside with a parked thread and deleted with a finished one, and graded oblivion in the wikis, `fade.py` |

## Take in

Recordings, mail and files you drop in all arrive in an inbox first. A recording's transcript goes to `Wikis/Meetings/raw/inbox/`, and mail and any other file go to a zone's `Inbox/`. Getting an item there is fetching, by hand, by a script or by your assistant's mail connector, and fetching decides nothing. Nothing waiting in an inbox can be committed: the pre-commit hook refuses it.

"Process the inbox" starts the [`intake`](concepts.md#intake) skill (the Garrick App's *Process the Inbox* button runs it in the background, and asks you only what does not settle, as questions in *Waiting on you now*), which files each item by the [Ways in](../template/System/skills/intake/SKILL.md#the-rules-for-where-things-go) rules. A conversation goes to the Meetings wiki, with its zone and parties. Something to read goes to the Knowledge wiki, with no parties. Material for a project goes into that project's `Sources/`, after the wall between the project's party and the material's parties has been checked. The parties come from the mail addresses, through the domains in the Parties table. Personal webmail never names a party, and when the addresses do not settle it, the assistant asks.

Each wiki keeps the item as it arrived in [`raw/`](concepts.md#raw), and writes its pages in `wiki/`. A raw record is never edited. Everything later rests on it, a correction included.

Detail: [Material coming in](architecture.md#material-coming-in), [The two memories](commands.md#the-two-memories), [Filing incoming material](principles.md#filing-incoming-material), [Ways in](extras/ways-in.md).

## Keep true

What one session writes down, a later one reads as fact. So what the workspace holds has to stay correct, and has to say where it came from.

- **Origin.** A [learned entry](concepts.md#learned-entry), an entry in the assistant's own memory or a row it added to a table in `System/context.md` or `System/names.md`, says where it came from: `user`, a note, or an inbound mail, transcript or page. What the assistant may write down without asking depends on what acting on it would do. A pointer or an alias goes ahead. A domain, a person's party or a destination waits for your yes. Something only a mail or a page said is never kept until you confirm it. `check.py` warns about an entry with no origin, and about one whose only source is inbound.
- **Correction.** "That's wrong, it's Y" runs `correct.py`, which lists every live copy of the wrong value and fixes them all on one yes. Live files then show only the new value, and git keeps the old one. A wiki page resting on a raw record that holds the old value is marked `corrected:` (its own text fixed) or `superseded:` (not fixable). See [Correction](concepts.md#correction).
- **Checks.** `check.py` tests the workspace against every rule a machine can check, `wiki_lint.py` finds orphan pages and broken links in a wiki, and `drift.py` compares a published deliverable with its shared copy. All three report and change nothing.
- **Nurture.** "Nurture the context" gathers what those three find for a scope and sorts it by what a fix needs. Mechanical fixes, such as a link broken by a rename, or the `parent:` line of a history file that the first history tool wrote in its older form, are applied on one yes. Attribution is asked one line at a time. A wall finding names the file and the wall, never the material. Judgment is reported with the page to open. "Review the context with me" works through what is left in a short session, and keeps a record so the next one goes on where it stopped. See [Nurturing the context](commands.md#nurturing-the-context).
- **The wrap.** "Wrap it" rewrites a thread's [Resume here](concepts.md#resume-here) block so it describes now, and adds a dated entry below it. `check.py` warns when a link or path in a live block leads nowhere.

## Use within walls

This stage gets the right part of the record to the task in front of you, and keeps each confidence on its own side.

- **Picking a thread back up.** "Open X" reads that thread's Resume here block and answers in two sentences: where it stands, and the next action. That block is the one thing a cold resume needs to read first. See [One resume point per thread](principles.md#one-resume-point-per-thread).
- **Questions go by subject.** "What did they say" goes to the Meetings wiki, "what does the research say" to the Knowledge wiki, "what do I owe them" to the zone's `Todo.md`, and "where am I on X" to the thread. A question that could belong to two zones is asked about. See [Where a question goes](../template/AGENTS.md#where-a-question-goes).
- **Briefs.** "Prep me for Acme" puts a brief together from the Meetings wiki. "What does my library say about X" answers from the Knowledge wiki.
- **The assistant's own memory holds pointers.** Where your assistant keeps a memory of its own, Garrick keeps one entry per live thread there, naming where its resume point is and nothing else. The note stays the record, so there is no second account of where a thread stands to go stale. A parked thread's pointer is set aside until it wakes: see [The memory index](#the-memory-index). See [Threads](../template/System/skills/threads/reference/wrap.md#the-rules-behind-this-request) in the rules.
- **The walls, at use.** Each zone is either **walled** or **open**. What is in a walled zone stays in that zone; what is in an open zone may go anywhere. Knowledge and System reach every zone, and a conversation that touches two walled zones is split before it is filed. Before material from a meeting goes into a project, the assistant compares the meeting's zone with the project's. Material of a walled zone stays out of another zone, paraphrase and summary included, and the assistant says only that something was held back. This is a rule the assistant follows, not code.
- **The walls, at commit.** The pre-commit hook in each zone refuses a file that links to, embeds or names the path of a file of another walled zone, or copies one whole. It has known gaps: a name typed by hand across zones passes, a clean paraphrase passes, and a chat reply or a mail sent is outside what it sees. See [What the check covers](principles.md#what-the-check-covers) and [What is enforced, and what is asked](enforced.md).

### The memory index

Where your assistant keeps a memory of its own, one file in it, `MEMORY.md`, lists every pointer, and every session in every zone loads it whole. Garrick keeps it short in three ways, all of them only where the `memory` setting in [`System/garrick-checks.json`](commands.md#check-settings) names that folder ([#664](https://github.com/iamvenuti/garrick/issues/664)):

- **A parked thread's pointer leaves it.** Parking a thread, from a session or from the page, moves its pointer file to a `parked/` folder beside the index and takes its line out; waking puts both back, the line as it was; finishing deletes the pointer. Nothing is committed, since the memory folder is outside the workspace. A pointer is the entry file the index links that names one hub or thread note by its path. When two entries name a note, or a file of the same name is already in `parked/`, the move holds, says so and overwrites nothing. A project parked whole keeps its threads' pointers, and its own moves only when none of its threads is live. An undo of a park leaves the pointer in `parked/`, and waking a thread that is already awake is refused: park the thread again, which moves nothing more, then wake it, and the pointer comes back. Put what a pointer knows, such as a boundary that is easy to get wrong, in the thread note too: a parked pointer is no longer loaded.
- **The index has a budget.** [`check.py`](enforced.md#the-memory-index-budget) warns when it is over 8,000 characters, as `System/rules.md` is, or when a line is over 160. The warning names the longest lines and the pointers of parked or done threads that are still in it. Nurture moves those pointers to `parked/` on one yes, and leaves a long line to you.
- **A hint stands in for the pointer.** With the optional [parked-thread hint](../extras/hooks/README.md#the-parked-thread-hint), a prompt that names a parked thread, or its party, gets one line saying so. Without it, "open X" still finds the parked thread by name, and when no name matches, the threads skill names the nearest parked thread after the nearest live ones.

## Let fade

What nobody uses any more should stop getting in the way, without being lost. Nothing in this stage deletes anything: what steps back can be brought back, and git keeps it all.

- **Distil.** At a wrap and when a meeting is filed, the `todo` skill's Distil changes one thread's open lines instead of only adding: it ticks what is done, rewords a line whose scope, date or person changed, adds only a new commitment, and moves a line the material drops to the expired archive, still open, with what replaced it. One line is one commitment, and a thread should hold five open lines at most (`todo-thread-cap`, a soft cap that warns and never drops a line). A line you moved to another section changes only on your yes in a session you are in; the nightly run writes the change as a proposal to `.todo-proposals.json`, which is never committed, and the morning digest lists it under *Proposed changes*. Say "apply the todo proposals" to go through them. **Follow up.** When the chase day of a `Waiting on` line comes, the `todo` skill looks, in code, for the reply in that zone's own Meetings pages and `Inbox/` (the items whose sender names the person), and the nightly run asks a small assistant call to judge what it found: *delivered*, *in part* or *no sign*. The result is in `.todo-followups.json`, which is never committed. It never ticks, redates or rewords a line: the status page's *Follow-ups* row offers *Tick it*, *Still waiting* and *Chase*, and "what am I waiting on" does the same look at once in a session.
- **Actions.** A line ticked before today moves from the zone's `Todo.md` to its `archive/`, at every wrap and at the nightly sweep. A line nobody triaged moves to the expired archive after `todo-expire-days`, 14 unless the [check settings](commands.md#check-settings) say otherwise.
- **Threads.** "Park X" sets a thread aside. It keeps its note and its Resume here block, drops out of "what's open", and comes back with "wake X". "Finish X" marks it `done`, turns its block into an outcome and lists it under the hub's `## Finished`. See [Status](concepts.md#status).
- **Memory.** The assistant's pointer to a thread moves to `parked/` when the thread is parked, comes back when it wakes, and is deleted when the thread finishes. See [The memory index](#the-memory-index).
- **The wikis: graded oblivion** ([#108](https://github.com/iamvenuti/garrick/issues/108)). Old meetings and reading fade out of working memory by age, kind and use. A page moves from working to dormant to archived, in folders inside its own wiki, its raw record with it, and every link into it follows. Both leave the index and Obsidian's default search. A question reaches dormant pages only when working pages fall short, and says so; archived ones are read when you ask. A page a live thread, a live project or an open action links, or one marked `pinned: true`, never fades, and a dormant page linked again comes back unless it is marked `superseded:`. The wall check still reads every page. "Fade the meetings" writes the list and moves nothing; on a wiki's first fade it stops there, and a later yes moves exactly that list. Nothing fades overnight. See [Fading](commands.md#fading).

## What runs on its own, and what waits for you

Core Garrick runs nothing on a schedule. The pre-commit hook runs on every commit, refuses what it is built to refuse, and lets the rest through. With the [scheduled jobs](extras/scheduled-jobs.md) extra, two sweeps also run each night: the todo sweep, and [Nurture's mechanical fixes](extras/scheduled-jobs.md#nurturing-the-context-each-night), each committing only the files it changed, named one by one.

Everything else waits for you: anything that would cross a wall, a domain, party or destination to be remembered, anything whose only source is a mail, a transcript or a page, a correction, an attribution, a judgment, and anything that leaves the machine. See [Autonomy](../template/System/rules.md#autonomy) in the rules, and [What an unattended assistant may do](extras/scheduled-jobs.md#what-an-unattended-assistant-may-do).
