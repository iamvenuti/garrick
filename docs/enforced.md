# What is enforced, and what is asked

Some of Garrick's protections are code: a script that refuses, whatever the assistant intends. The rest are rules the assistant is asked to follow, and they hold only as well as it follows them. This page says which is which, so you can judge each one for what it is.

## The wall check's known gaps

The wall check catches specific signs of material crossing a declared wall. It does not catch everything, and one gap is known and open:

- **A party whose tag is an ordinary word.** A tag such as `anchor` or `delta` is a name form too, so once a wall is declared against that party, every note on the near side that uses the word in its ordinary sense is refused. Nothing on the near side names that party, so the rule that clears a person who is a party on both sides does not reach it: the near side is simply writing English. In practice the wall cannot be declared without refusing ordinary notes, and a wall left out of the table holds nothing back. Until this is settled, give such a party a tag that is not a common word before you declare its wall. Described in [a comment on #98](https://github.com/iamvenuti/garrick/issues/98#issuecomment-6174966795).

A person who is a party, and also on the near side of a wall as themselves, was a second gap until [#98](https://github.com/iamvenuti/garrick/issues/98): the People row under the near-side party cleared their full name but not the party's tag, which for a one-to-one client is often the surname. That row now clears the tag with the name, and a refusal that matched a tag rather than a name says which, `names Dana Rossi (as "rossi")`.

Two things the commit hook does not read, which only the full check reports (#340): a Resume here or checkpoint path that lands in another zone, and any change made in a repository with no hook. The workspace's root repository has none by default, so a commit of `System/context.md` there is read by the staged walls check only when Nurture or `correct.py` makes it.

Beyond this, the check has limits by design: a clean paraphrase, wording moved rather than copied, the contents of Word, PowerPoint and PDF files, chat replies and anything sent are outside what it reads. [What the check covers](principles.md#what-the-check-covers) sets them out. A clean result means the implemented checks found nothing, not that nothing crossed.
