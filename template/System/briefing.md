# Briefing

What deserves attention when you are caught up on what changed while you were away. `System/tools/briefing.py` gathers what changed, thread by thread, from the notes, their history and the Todo list, and decides nothing; this file is the judgment a person would add, read by the `threads` skill each time it writes a catch-up. A thing this file says to raise comes first in its thread's section, and the overview names it when it needs you.

This file is Garrick's, as shipped, and an update replaces it. Read `System/briefing.local.md` next, when it exists: it holds this workspace's own judgment, in plain words, and wins where the two differ. Change a rule there and nowhere else.

## Raise first

- A decision, with who took it and why, in the words it was taken in where the wording was the point.
- What now waits on you: a question someone put to you, an action of yours that came due or went late, a chase whose day has come.
- What changed the plan: a deadline moved, a scope changed, a next action replaced by a different one, a thread parked, woken or finished.
- What someone else did in your thread: a commit by another person or another session, work saved and not committed, a checkpoint nobody has wrapped.
- What to look at before you carry on: the file the Live artifact row now names, a deliverable made in the period, an entry that says something was left out on purpose.
- A gap in the records: work that moved with no dated entry to say why, a resume point older than the thread's last commit, an action that could belong to two threads. Say it as a gap, and never fill it in with a guess.

## Say in a line

- Steady progress: a draft taken further, a line ticked that was only bookkeeping.
- Several commits of one piece of work: say the work once, not each commit.

## Leave out

- The threads that did not change, beyond how many there are, and a parked thread nobody named.
- Housekeeping: a commit that only swept or tidied the Todo list, renamed or moved files, or fixed a typo.
- Anything the records do not hold. A catch-up reports what the notes, their history and the list say, not what probably happened in between.
- What a meeting said. A meeting the thread note links is named by that link and nothing more: the note's own entries already carry what passed the wall check.

## Never

- A catch-up never ranks one client, party or person above another, and never judges how well anyone did.
- It changes nothing: no tick, no wrap, no commit. Saving it is a separate yes, and only for a catch-up that covers one project.
