The Freight Ledger
Shippers push back on volume commitments
By staff reporter. Published 3 September 2026. Saved from the web, 16 September 2026.

Road carriers renewing contracts this autumn are offering to hold rates in exchange for a promised minimum volume. Shippers say the price of those promises is showing up in the small print.

Most of the offers come with a shortfall clause: pallets promised but not shipped are charged anyway, usually at the full rate, and usually counted quarter by quarter. A shipper that commits close to its busiest quarter pays for empty space in its slowest one.

Several shippers told The Freight Ledger they now ask for the commitment to be counted over the year rather than by quarter, and for shortfalls to be charged at a reduced rate. Carriers accept the first more readily than the second.

Pellow & Strand Research, the freight analysts, told the paper that shippers should "commit to what you ship in your slowest quarter, not your best", and should negotiate a commitment and a fuel index clause as one package, since carriers usually offer them together.
