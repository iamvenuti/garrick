Corbel Supply Institute
Second Sources: Qualifying an Alternative Supplier
Practice guide. Published 20 November 2025. Downloaded from the Institute's website, 20 September 2026.

Why this guide

We surveyed 210 manufacturers with between 50 and 1,000 employees in 2025. Sixty-two percent had at least one part from a single supplier that would stop a production line within a month if it stopped arriving.

How long it takes

Qualifying a second source for a machined, cast or moulded part took a median of eleven weeks, from first drawings to an approved first batch. Rubber and polymer parts took longest, because of ageing and pressure tests.

The steps

1. Send drawings and the material specification.
2. Test samples against the specification.
3. Inspect the first production batch against the drawings (first-article inspection).
4. Place a trial order before moving regular volume.

While you wait

Hold extra stock of the part, a bridge, until the second source is approved. Size it to the qualification time, not to the usual reorder cycle.

Keep the second source warm

A second source does not need half the volume. Respondents that kept an 80/20 split held on to their second supplier; those that placed a token order once a year usually lost it.

Methodology: online survey of 210 manufacturers in seven European countries, January to March 2025, and interviews with 18 purchasing managers.
